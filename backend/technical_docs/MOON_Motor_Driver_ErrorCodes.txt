CRITICAL ERROR CODES (Device Protection Shutdown)
================================================================================

ERROR 01: OVER CURRENT PROTECTION
- Description: Motor current exceeded safe operating limits
- Severity: CRITICAL - Immediate shutdown
- Current Limits:
  - MOON-150: 150A continuous, 300A peak (2 seconds)
  - MOON-300: 300A continuous, 600A peak (2 seconds)
  - MOON-600: 600A continuous, 1200A peak (2 seconds)
- Symptoms: Motor stops abruptly, driver LED flashes red, error logged
- Root Causes:
  1. MOTOR OVERLOAD: Load exceeds motor capacity
  2. MECHANICAL JAM: Motor shaft stuck or obstructed
  3. PHASE SHORT: Short circuit in motor windings or cables
  4. INCORRECT PARAMETERS: Motor parameters mis-configured
  5. BRAKE ENGAGED: Mechanical brake not releasing
- Diagnosis Procedure:
  1. Disconnect motor from mechanical load
  2. Check motor shaft rotates freely by hand
  3. Measure motor phase resistance: Should be 0.05-0.5 Ohms per phase
  4. Inspect motor cables for damage, pinching, or cuts
  5. Verify motor parameters match motor datasheet
- Resolution:
  IMMEDIATE:
  - Power off driver and motor
  - Do not restart until cause identified
  MECHANICAL ISSUE:
  - Clear obstruction from motor shaft
  - Check gearbox (if present) for seized bearings
  - Verify load is within motor specifications
  ELECTRICAL ISSUE:
  - Replace damaged motor cables
  - Check motor winding resistance (call motor manufacturer)
  - Verify all phase connections tight and correct (U, V, W order)
  CONFIGURATION:
  - Re-configure motor parameters: pole pairs, max current, Kv
  - Reduce acceleration rate in motion profile
  - Lower maximum torque limit to 80% of motor rating
- Error Reset: Power cycle driver, press RESET button, or send CAN reset command
- If Error Persists: Motor or driver hardware failure - professional service required
- Cost Impact: Motor replacement $500-2000, Driver replacement $800-3000

ERROR 02: OVER VOLTAGE PROTECTION
- Description: DC bus voltage exceeded maximum safe level
- Severity: CRITICAL - Immediate shutdown
- Voltage Limits:
  - MOON-150: Max 60V DC
  - MOON-300: Max 80V DC  
  - MOON-600: Max 100V DC
- Symptoms: Driver shuts down during deceleration or when motor acts as generator
- Root Causes:
  1. REGENERATIVE BRAKING: Motor generates power during deceleration
  2. EXTERNAL VOLTAGE SPIKE: Power supply voltage surge
  3. BRAKE RESISTOR FAILURE: Brake resistor disconnected or burned out
  4. RAPID DECELERATION: Too-fast deceleration creates high voltage
- Diagnosis:
  1. Measure DC bus voltage with multimeter at driver terminals
  2. Check brake resistor: Should be 5-50 Ohms depending on model
  3. Inspect brake resistor connections (often screw terminals)
  4. Monitor voltage during deceleration with oscilloscope
- Resolution:
  BRAKE RESISTOR:
  - Verify brake resistor connected (required for inertial loads)
  - Check resistor value matches driver requirements:
    - MOON-150: 10-20 Ohms, minimum 100W
    - MOON-300: 5-10 Ohms, minimum 200W
    - MOON-600: 2.5-5 Ohms, minimum 400W
  - Replace burned resistor (appears blackened or cracked)
  MOTION PROFILE:
  - Increase deceleration time: Change from 100ms to 500ms+
  - Reduce maximum speed to decrease kinetic energy
  - Enable S-curve acceleration profile (smoother)
  POWER SUPPLY:
  - Add capacitor bank to bus (470uF-1000uF per 100W load)
  - Install voltage clamp or TVS diode (talk to electrician)
  - Use isolated power supply (reduce ground loop voltage spikes)
- Configuration: Access via CAN bus or USB interface (MOON Studio software)
- Expected Resolution Time: 30-60 minutes (hardware), 10-20 minutes (software)

ERROR 03: UNDER VOLTAGE LOCKOUT
- Description: Supply voltage dropped below minimum operating threshold
- Severity: ERROR - Device non-functional
- Minimum Voltages:
  - MOON-150: 12V DC minimum, 24V nominal
  - MOON-300: 18V DC minimum, 48V nominal
  - MOON-600: 24V DC minimum, 72V nominal
- Symptoms: Driver won't enable, motor won't run, power LED dim or off
- Root Causes:
  1. WEAK POWER SUPPLY: Undersized supply for application
  2. VOLTAGE DROP: Long cable runs or undersized wire gauge
  3. POOR CONNECTIONS: Loose terminals, corroded contacts
  4. SUPPLY OVERLOAD: Other devices on same supply drawing too much
- Diagnosis:
  1. Measure voltage at power supply output: Should match supply rating ±5%
  2. Measure voltage at driver input: Should be within 1V of supply output
  3. Difference >1V indicates cable problem
  4. Measure voltage under load (motor running): Shouldn't drop >10%
- Resolution:
  POWER SUPPLY:
  - Calculate required supply capacity: Motor watts + 30% margin
  - Upgrade supply if undersized
  - Typical: 200W motor needs 300W supply (considering efficiency)
  WIRING:
  - Cable gauge requirements:
    - <1m: 18 AWG minimum
    - 1-3m: 16 AWG minimum  
    - 3-5m: 14 AWG minimum
    - >5m: 12 AWG or use voltage booster
  - Use stranded copper wire, not solid
  - Twist positive and negative wires together (reduces inductance)
  CONNECTIONS:
  - Clean all terminals with contact cleaner
  - Tighten all screw terminals to specified torque (typically 0.8-1.2 Nm)
  - Use star washers to prevent loosening from vibration
  - Check for green corrosion on copper (indicates moisture)
- Prevention: Periodic connection check (quarterly), use ferrules on stranded wire

ERROR 08: OVER TEMPERATURE WARNING
- Description: Driver or motor temperature approaching limits
- Severity: WARNING → ERROR if continues
- Temperature Limits:
  - Driver: 85°C warning, 95°C shutdown
  - Motor: 100°C warning, 120°C shutdown (typical)
- Symptoms: Reduced performance, torque limiting active, eventual shutdown
- Root Causes:
  1. INSUFFICIENT COOLING: Blocked airflow, no heatsink, high ambient
  2. OVERWORK: Continuous high-power operation
  3. HIGH AMBIENT: Operating in hot environment (>40°C)
  4. DAMAGED FAN: Cooling fan failed or obstructed
- Diagnosis:
  1. Feel driver heatsink - should be warm but not painful to touch
  2. Check fan operation - should hear airflow
  3. Measure ambient temperature - should be <40°C
  4. Monitor driver temperature via CAN bus (MOON Studio software)
  5. Check motor temperature - use infrared thermometer or thermocouple
- Resolution:
  IMMEDIATE (If >85°C):
  - Reduce load or speed by 50%
  - Increase deceleration time (reduces peak power)
  - Add cooling fan to driver (12V, 80-120mm, 0.5-1.0 CFM)
  INSTALLATION:
  - Mount driver vertically (heatsink fins vertical)
  - Ensure 50mm clearance on all sides for airflow
  - Mount in coolest area of enclosure
  - Add ventilation holes in enclosure (intake bottom, exhaust top)
  - Consider external heatsink (available from MOON: part MOON-HS-xxx)
  OPERATIONAL:
  - Duty cycle: 80% max (alternate work/rest periods)
  - Reduce continuous current limit to 70% of motor rating
  - Lower maximum speed by 20% (reduces losses)
  - Enable "Energy Save Mode" during idle periods
  ENVIRONMENTAL:
  - If ambient >40°C: Add air conditioning or move equipment
  - Check for heat sources: motors, transformers, heaters nearby
  - Use thermal paste between driver and mounting surface
- Expected Resolution: Immediate (reduce load), 1-2 hours (add cooling)

ERROR 10: HALL SENSOR ERROR
- Description: Motor hall sensor signals invalid or missing
- Severity: ERROR - Motor won't run or runs erratically
- Symptoms: Motor vibrates but doesn't rotate, rough running, error at startup
- Root Causes:
  1. DISCONNECTED HALL CABLE: Cable loose or unplugged
  2. DAMAGED CABLE: Hall sensor wire cut or pinched
  3. WRONG SEQUENCE: Hall sensors connected in wrong order
  4. FAILED SENSOR: Hall sensor component failure in motor
  5. ELECTRICAL NOISE: EMI interfering with hall signals
- Diagnosis:
  1. Check hall cable connections at both driver and motor
  2. Measure hall sensor voltage: Should be 0V and 5V alternating
  3. Rotate motor by hand - hall signals should change every 60° (for 6-step commutation)
  4. Verify hall cable isn't routed parallel to power cables (causes noise)
  5. Check hall sensor power: Should be 5V ±0.5V from driver
- Resolution:
  CONNECTIONS:
  - Reconnect hall cable firmly (usually 5 or 6 pin connector)
  - Check pin assignment: H1, H2, H3, +5V, GND (refer to motor datasheet)
  - Verify connector not damaged (bent pins, cracks)
  CABLE:
  - Replace damaged hall cable
  - Use shielded cable for hall sensors (shield to ground at driver end only)
  - Maximum cable length: 3m (longer needs signal conditioning)
  - Route hall cable away from power cables (minimum 10cm separation)
  HALL SEQUENCE:
  - Try different hall wire order if motor runs backward or rough:
  - Swap H1 and H3 (changes direction)
  - Try permutations until smooth operation
  - Document correct sequence for future reference
  SENSOR FAILURE:
  - If all above fail: Motor hall sensors failed (requires motor disassembly)
  - Alternative: Configure driver for sensorless mode (if supported)
  - Contact motor manufacturer for hall sensor replacement kit
- Configuration: MOON Studio → Motor Setup → Hall Sensor Configuration
- Cost: Hall cable $20-50, Motor repair $200-500

ERROR 14: CAN BUS COMMUNICATION TIMEOUT
- Description: No CAN messages received within timeout period
- Severity: WARNING → ERROR (driver may enter safe state)
- Symptoms: Motor stops responding, commands ignored, heartbeat lost
- Root Causes:
  1. CABLE DISCONNECTED: CAN cable loose or unplugged
  2. TERMINATION MISSING: 120 Ohm termination resistors not installed
  3. BAUDRATE MISMATCH: Driver and controller using different speeds
  4. BUS OVERLOAD: Too many devices or too much traffic
  5. CONTROLLER FAILURE: Main controller stopped sending
- Diagnosis:
  1. Check CAN cable connections (CAN-H and CAN-L wires)
  2. Verify termination: Measure resistance between CAN-H and CAN-L with bus unpowered (should be 60 Ohms if two 120 Ohm terminators present)
  3. Check baudrate setting: Common rates are 250 kbps, 500 kbps, 1 Mbps
  4. Use CAN analyzer tool to monitor bus traffic
  5. Verify driver node ID is unique (no conflicts)
- Resolution:
  PHYSICAL:
  - Reconnect CAN cables (twisted pair, typically orange/yellow)
  - Install 120 Ohm termination resistors at both ends of bus
  - Use proper CAN cable (120 Ohm characteristic impedance)
  - Maximum bus length without repeater: 40m at 1 Mbps, 500m at 100 kbps
  CONFIGURATION:
  - Match baudrate on all devices (check with MOON Studio)
  - Set appropriate timeout: 100-1000ms depending on application
  - Verify node ID: Each device needs unique ID (1-127)
  - Enable heartbeat message in controller (typically 10-100ms interval)
  SOFTWARE:
  - Reduce message rate if bus saturated (>70% utilization)
  - Prioritize critical messages (motion commands)
  - Implement proper error handling in controller software
- Tools Required: Multimeter, CAN analyzer (optional but helpful)
- Expected Resolution: 15-45 minutes

ERROR 15: ENCODER ERROR
- Description: Position encoder signal invalid or lost
- Severity: ERROR - Position control not possible
- Symptoms: Position drift, inaccurate positioning, following error
- Root Causes:
  1. ENCODER CABLE: Disconnected, damaged, or noisy
  2. ENCODER FAILURE: Encoder hardware failed
  3. MECHANICAL: Encoder shaft coupling loose or broken
  4. ELECTRICAL NOISE: EMI corrupting encoder signals
  5. WRONG PARAMETERS: Encoder resolution mis-configured
- Diagnosis:
  1. Check encoder cable connection (typically 8-15 pins)
  2. Verify encoder power supply: 5V ±5% or 12V ±10% depending on encoder
  3. Monitor encoder signals with oscilloscope:
     - Incremental: Should see square waves on A and B channels
     - Absolute: Should see serial data on clock and data lines
  4. Check mechanical coupling: Shaft should be firmly attached
  5. Verify encoder count matches motor rotation (monitor in MOON Studio)
- Resolution:
  CABLE:
  - Reconnect encoder cable securely
  - Use shielded cable for encoder (shield grounded at driver end)
  - Keep encoder cable separate from power cables (20cm minimum)
  - Maximum length: Incremental 10m, Absolute SSI 50m
  MECHANICAL:
  - Tighten encoder coupling (typically set screw or clamp collar)
  - Check alignment: Encoder shaft and motor shaft should be concentric (±0.1mm)
  - Verify no axial load on encoder (can damage bearings)
  CONFIGURATION:
  - Set correct encoder resolution (PPR - pulses per revolution):
    - Common values: 1000, 2000, 2500, 4096, 10000 PPR
  - Verify quadrature mode: x1, x2, or x4 multiplication
  - Check direction: Reverse if position decreases when motor turns CW
  ENCODER FAILURE:
  - Test encoder separately: Apply 5V power, check output signals
  - If no output: Encoder failed - replacement needed ($50-500 depending on type)
- Prevention: Protect encoder from shock, vibration, and moisture

ERROR 22: GATE DRIVER FAULT
- Description: Internal gate driver circuit fault detected
- Severity: CRITICAL - Driver inoperable
- Symptoms: Driver won't enable, all phases disabled, power stage protection
- Root Causes:
  1. MOSFET FAILURE: Power MOSFET short circuit or damaged
  2. BOOTSTRAP CAPACITOR: Bootstrap supply failed
  3. DESATURATION: MOSFET desaturation detected (over-current event)
  4. SHOOT-THROUGH: High and low side MOSFETs conducted simultaneously
  5. DRIVER IC FAILURE: Gate driver IC component failure
- Diagnosis:
  WARNING: This is an internal hardware fault - limited user diagnosis possible
  1. Power cycle driver completely (off 60 seconds)
  2. Disconnect motor from driver (remove U, V, W connections)
  3. Attempt to enable driver with no motor connected
  4. If error persists: Internal hardware failure confirmed
  5. Check for visible damage: burned components, cracked PCB
- Resolution:
  USER ACTIONS:
  - Power off driver immediately
  - DO NOT attempt to restart repeatedly (can cause further damage)
  - Document what happened before failure:
    - What was motor doing?
    - Any unusual sounds or smells?
    - Any recent changes to setup?
  PROFESSIONAL SERVICE REQUIRED:
  - This fault requires factory repair or replacement
  - Typical repair: $400-800 (50-70% of new driver cost)
  - Repair time: 2-4 weeks
  - Consider replacement if driver >5 years old
  PREVENTION FOR REPLACEMENT:
  - Ensure motor parameters correct to avoid overcurrent
  - Add current limiting in motion controller
  - Use soft-start feature (ramp up gradually)
  - Check for intermittent short circuits in motor cables
- Contact MOON Service: service@moonmotion.com or +1-555-MOON-TEC
- Warranty: 24 months from purchase - free replacement if manufacturing defect

ERROR 25: EEPROM ERROR
- Description: Configuration memory read/write failure
- Severity: WARNING → ERROR
- Symptoms: Settings not saved, defaults loaded on power-up, parameter errors
- Root Causes:
  1. MEMORY WEAR: EEPROM write cycles exhausted (typical life: 100,000 cycles)
  2. POWER INTERRUPTION: Power lost during write operation
  3. CORRUPTION: Data corruption from electrical noise or bit errors
  4. HARDWARE FAILURE: EEPROM chip failure
- Diagnosis:
  1. Check if settings persist after power cycle
  2. Try saving configuration multiple times
  3. Read EEPROM write cycle count (if available in MOON Studio)
  4. Review error log for frequency of EEPROM errors
- Resolution:
  SOFTWARE:
  - Restore factory defaults: Hold RESET button for 15 seconds
  - Reconfigure all parameters manually
  - Save configuration and verify it persists
  - If successful: Previous configuration was corrupted
  HARDWARE:
  - If error repeats after multiple attempts: Hardware failure
  - Backup configuration to PC (MOON Studio → Save Config)
  - Contact service for EEPROM replacement (simple repair, $150-250)
  PREVENTION:
  - Don't save configuration unnecessarily (reduces write cycles)
  - Use parameter sets for different modes instead of frequent saves
  - Ensure stable power supply during configuration changes
- Temporary Workaround: Upload configuration at startup from controller

ERROR 30: BRAKE RESISTOR ERROR
- Description: Brake resistor overheating or disconnected
- Severity: WARNING → CRITICAL
- Symptoms: Over-voltage errors during deceleration, reduced braking capability
- Root Causes:
  1. RESISTOR OVERHEATING: Excessive regenerative energy
  2. DISCONNECTED: Resistor not connected or wire broken
  3. WRONG VALUE: Incorrect resistance value used
  4. INSUFFICIENT WATTAGE: Resistor can't handle power dissipation
- Diagnosis:
  1. Check resistor connection (usually screw terminals labeled BR+/BR-)
  2. Measure resistance: Should match specified value ±10%
  3. Feel resistor temperature: Should be warm but <100°C
  4. Calculate power dissipation:
     - Formula: P = (V² × duty_cycle) / R
     - Example: 48V bus, 10% braking = (48² × 0.1) / 10Ω = 23W
  5. Compare to resistor wattage rating
- Resolution:
  SELECTION:
  - Use resistor value recommended in driver manual:
    - MOON-150: 10-20Ω, 100W minimum
    - MOON-300: 5-10Ω, 200W minimum
    - MOON-600: 2.5-5Ω, 400W minimum
  - Lower resistance = more braking capability but higher current
  INSTALLATION:
  - Mount resistor in free air with 50mm clearance
  - Use wire rated for resistor temperature (typically 200°C silicone wire)
  - Mount away from heat-sensitive components
  - Consider aluminum housing for heat dissipation
  THERMAL PROTECTION:
  - Add thermal switch to resistor (NC contact, opens at 120°C)
  - Connect to driver safety input
  - If resistor overheats: Driver automatically limits braking
  ALTERNATIVES:
  - Reduce deceleration rate (less braking energy)
  - Increase deceleration time (spreads energy over time)
  - Return energy to power supply (if supply accepts regeneration)
- Expected Cost: Brake resistor $20-100 depending on wattage
